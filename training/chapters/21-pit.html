Pit troubleshooting.

Vision is broken and the match is in 10 minutes. Start with what you see. This page walks you to the fix, quickest step first, and tells you how to know it worked.

Stuck camera? Don't reboot.A reboot doesn't cut USB power, so it can't fix a stuck Thriftiest Cam. It has hung the Jetson twice. Replug the camera, or power-cycle the robot.
Quickest thing first.Look before you change anything. Most problems are a plug, a name or a setting.
One change at a time.Then check again. Write down what you changed, so a mentor can follow it later.

This is the action guide. Why the Jetson behaves the way it does (the watchdogs, the health topics, the known issues) is in chapter 16.

    The 60-second health check

    Before you start fixing, or when you're not sure what's wrong, look at these in order. The first three need only a browser.

    1. Match ReadyOpen Match Ready in PhotonVision's sidebar (photonvision-52). It shows a big READY or NOT READY, with anything that needs attention first. It runs the same checks as health-check.sh, every 30 s while the page is open (or press Check again), so the page and SSH always agree. Each camera gets a live tile: pipeline number and name, fps, latency, targets in view, and whether it's calibrated at its resolution. Every check is underneath, by section.
    2. The dashboardOpen http://10.85.15.15:5800 (on the bench, over the USB-C cable: 192.168.55.1:5800). Every camera (TopLeft, TopRight, BottomLeft, BottomRight) should show about 120 fps. Hold a tag up to each one: it should get a box. About 30 fps while the robot is disabled is normal. That's idle mode (photonvision-49): while connected to a disabled robot, each camera processes 30 frames a second, and it's back to full rate within 5 ms of enable. The dashboard says "Robot disabled: cameras idle at 30 fps, full speed on enable"; press Full speed on it to test at full rate, or switch off Idle while the robot is disabled in Settings > Robot state. It's saved across restarts, so health-check.sh warns while it's off: switch it back on. The video in the browser is capped at 30 fps on purpose too, to save CPU. Judge by the fps number, not by how smooth the video looks.
    3. The Settings pageCPU Throttling should say None. OVER-CURRENT means the power sagged (check the battery). HIGH TEMP means it's too hot (check the fan and airflow). The GPU Usage chart below it read 10–13% with two cameras on the bench; a flat 0% means no detector is running.
    4. The robot's viewIn a NetworkTables viewer on the robot side, /photonvision/<camera>/health/fps should be about 120 for every camera. If a camera's health/problem has text in it, it's telling you what to do. /photonvision/jetson/throttle should say None, and heartbeat should keep counting up.
    5. The health checkStill unsure? SSH in and run health-check.sh. It checks PhotonVision, the detector, every camera, the robot connection, storage and the Jetson itself, and ends with READY or NOT READY. Every FAIL line says what to do. It also fails a camera whose port is empty, and the SSD if it reports any media errors (replace the drive). It reports the event pipeline, the far-tag search, and any camera driver setting that's live now but would be lost at the next boot.

    The live log and its "971 stats" line
    
  
    
    
    
      
    journalctl -u photonvision -f shows PhotonVision's log as it's written (Ctrl+C to stop). It's busy. The most useful lines come from our GPU detector: once a second, one 971 stats line for every AprilTag camera.
    
      
    Add | grep "971 stats" to see only those. Four cameras means four lines a second. If a camera's line is missing, that camera isn't reaching the detector.
    
      
    Every 10 s there's also a 971 jpeg line: which JPEG decoder is running (the NVJPG hardware, or libjpeg-turbo on the CPU) and whether its checks still match.

    Restart, replug, reboot or power-cycle?
    
  
    Four ways to "turn it off and on again". They fix different things, and one of them can make things worse.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    WhatHowFixesBack in
    
      
    Restart PhotonVisionSettings › Device Control › Restart Software, or sudo systemctl restart photonvisionSettings that didn't take, a camera sending bad frames, a detector that won't load. Safe any time.a few seconds
    
      
    Replug one cameraPull its USB plug, wait 2 s, push it back into its own labeled portA stuck or missing camera. The other cameras keep running.~1 s after it's back in
    
      
    Power-cycle the robotRobot off, wait a few seconds, onEverything a replug fixes, for every camera at once. Also a Jetson that won't boot.~20 s to detecting tags
    
      
    Reboot the JetsonReboot Device button, or sudo rebootAlmost nothing a restart doesn't. It doesn't cut USB power, so stuck cameras stay stuck, and it has hung twice.avoid

    Useful buttons and commands
    
  
    
    
    In PhotonVision's Settings › Device Control: Restart Software is always safe. View Logs (or press the ` key on any page) shows the live log. Download Logs saves it to the laptop for a mentor. Avoid Reboot Device when a camera is stuck. The full course lists the Linux commands for the Jetson itself.
    
  
    
  
    
    
    From a laptop on the robot network. On the bench, swap 10.85.15.15 for 192.168.55.1. The first one logs you in; the rest run on the Jetson.
    
    
    
      
    ssh -i ~/.ssh/jetson_ed25519 spectrum3847@10.85.15.15
    Log in to the Jetson. Type exit to leave.
    
      
    ~/SpectrumJetson/scripts/jetson/health-check.sh 4
    The readiness report, expecting 4 cameras. No sudo needed.
    
      
    journalctl -u photonvision -f | grep "971 stats"
    Live fps and detect time per camera, once a second. Drop the grep for the whole log.
    
      
    lsusb -t
    Which USB devices the Jetson can see. Each camera is two Class=Video lines under the hub; "Port 1" to "Port 4" are USB ports 2.1 to 2.4. They should say 480M. A camera at 1.5M is a bad cable or plug.
    
      
    journalctl -k --since "-10 min" | grep usb
    USB errors from the kernel. error -110 and "unable to enumerate" = not answering; error -71 = data wires; "Not enough bandwidth" = USB budget. (sudo dmesg shows the same.)
    
      
    ~/SpectrumJetson/scripts/jetson/usb-bandwidth.py
    What each camera reserves on the shared USB 2.0 budget, which port failed, and the fix.
    
      
    sudo systemctl restart photonvision
    Restart PhotonVision. Fine any time; same as Restart Software.
    
      
    journalctl -u photonvision -b -1 -n 200
    The end of the last boot's log, e.g. after a power cut. The log is kept on the SSD across power cuts, except while quiet mode has it in RAM after a match (below).
    
      
    ~/SpectrumJetson/scripts/jetson/ro-root.sh status
    Is the system partition read-only? It should be at competition, and anything installed while it's on is gone at the next boot. To update: ro-root.sh off, update, ro-root.sh on. Each one reboots the Jetson, so not while a camera is stuck.
    
      
    sudo reboot
    Avoid if any camera is stuck. Power-cycle the robot instead.
    
    
    
    
    
      Getting logs off the Jetson for later
      
    
        
      
          
    • From the browser: Settings › Device Control › Download Logs saves PhotonVision's system log as photonvision-journalctl.txt. The log window (View Logs, or the ` key) has its own Download button, for what this browser has received.
      • 
          
    • Export Settings downloads a zip of every setting. PhotonVision's docs say it includes the old log files too. It's also the backup to take before changing anything big.
      • 
          
    • Over SSH: journalctl -u photonvision --since "30 min ago" > pv.log, then copy it to the laptop with scp.
      • 
          
    • Mode changes are logged. PhotonVision writes a "ROBOT TRANSITIONED MODES!" line when the robot is enabled, disabled or switches between auto and teleop, with the match number when the field is attached. Search for it to find a match in a long log.
      • 
          
    • Camera settings are in the robot log (/photonvision/<camera>/settingsJson), so you can check what a camera was set to in any match without touching the Jetson.
      • 
        
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
    Things that look broken but aren't
    
  
    
    
      
      
    • 30 fps while disabled. Idle mode, on purpose (above). Any enable brings full speed back within 5 ms.
      • 
      
    • Rewind stopped recording after a match. That's quiet mode (photonvision-56). Power cuts only damage an SSD while it's being written, and the robot is switched off a few minutes after every match. So when a match ends (field connected, teleop, then disabled), or after 60 s disabled, the Jetson stops writing: Rewind stops, PhotonVision's log file closes (the log still shows in the UI), the system log moves to RAM, and the scratch partition goes read-only. The cameras keep running and tags are still found. Any enable ends it. A recording someone asks for always wins: Record now in Settings › Rewind, field calibration, or robot code's /photonvision/rewind/record keeps quiet mode off for as long as it records.
      • 
      
    • A dashboard tab's video went blank while you looked away. A tab behind another tab, or in a minimised window, disconnects its streams so the Jetson stops encoding them (photonvision-48). They come back when you look at the tab again.
      • 
      
    • Every camera at about 60 fps, on a fanless Jetson. The thermal cap (photonvision-57): at 95 °C every camera is capped at 60 fps until the chip is under 88 °C. Match Ready and Settings › Robot state show it. Give it air, or let it cool between matches.
      • 
    
    
    
    Getting a good setup back
    
    
    Every change in the dashboard is saved right away, with no undo. If a good setup got lost, open the Settings page's Settings snapshots card and Restore the last good one: every camera's pipelines, settings and calibrations come back, and PhotonVision restarts (back in about 10 s). It saves the current settings first as "Before restoring …", so a restore can be undone. PhotonVision saves a snapshot by itself the first time the field connects each day, so there's always one from the start of the event (photonvision-53). Settings from any earlier match are also in that match's robot log (settingsJson).

    Chapter 21 recap
    
    
      
      
    • Start from the symptom. The quickest check is always first.
      • 
      
    • Look first: Match Ready (READY), dashboard fps (~120, or 30 while disabled), CPU Throttling (None), the health topics, then health-check.sh.
      • 
      
    • A stuck camera needs its power cut: replug it or power-cycle the robot. Don't reboot the Jetson.
      • 
      
    • Restarting PhotonVision is always safe.

    Chapter 21 recap
    
    
      
      
    • Symptom first: the finder above covers the problems we've actually seen, with the fix and how to confirm it.
      • 
      
    • The 60-second check: Match Ready, dashboard fps (30 while disabled is idle mode), CPU Throttling, /photonvision/<camera>/health/ and /photonvision/jetson/, then health-check.sh 4 over SSH.
      • 
      
    • 971 stats lines: fps, detect time, tags and decision margin, per camera, every second.
      • 
      
    • Restart PhotonVision freely. Replug or power-cycle for stuck cameras. Avoid sudo reboot when cameras are stuck.
      • 
      
    • Logs: Download Logs, Export Settings, or journalctl over SSH. The log survives power cuts, except during quiet mode after a match.
      • 
      
    • A lost setup: restore a settings snapshot. Recording stops after a match: quiet mode, ended by any enable.
